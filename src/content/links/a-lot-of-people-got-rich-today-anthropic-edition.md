Ashley Capoot, Caleigh Keating, Samantha Subin, Greg Iacurci, Leslie Picker, and Robert Frank, reporting for CNBC on the Anthropic IPO throughout Thursday’s trading session:

> Anthropic jumped 17% in its Nasdaq debut on Thursday in a seminal moment for artificial intelligence, after the company completed the biggest initial public offering on record, topping SpaceX’s historic debut in June. The stock closed at $97.17 per share.
> 
> The Claude creator, trading under the ticker “ANT,” opened at $111.50, after the company raised $81.3 billion in the IPO. Anthropic sold 980 million shares at $83 a piece on Wednesday, lifting the size and price of its offering.
> 
> At Thursday’s close, Anthropic had a fully diluted market cap of roughly $1.8 trillion.

Consequently, a lot of people made a lot of money today. 

The banks:

> Wall Street banks are set to reap a huge windfall from Anthropic’s IPO, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley among the biggest earners. [...] The company disclosed in its latest SEC filing that it agreed to pay the banks $550 million, surpassing the roughly $500 million payday banks took in from SpaceX’s IPO in June.

Big tech:

> Anthropic investors Amazon and Google own roughly 28% of the company’s outstanding capital stock following the offering. 
> 
> The company said Amazon owns 14.7%, while Google owns 13.7%, according to its prospectus.

The venture capitalists and their LPs:

> Menlo Ventures partner Matt Murphy, an early Anthropic investor, said the firm’s aggregate position in the AI company is around $25 billion.
>
> “Clearly the most epic return for Menlo Ventures in our history, and one of the top handful in the history of venture capital,” Murphy told CNBC in an interview. “We feel grateful. We have over five LPs that we’ve delivered over a billion dollars of returns to, and at the end of the day, that’s really our job.”

The actor-cum-venture capitalist:

> Ashton Kutcher congratulated Dario Amodei and “the whole team at Anthropic” in a post on X shortly before the AI company’s first trade.
>
> Kutcher co-founded Sound Ventures, a Los Angeles-based venture capital firm, which invested in Anthropic in 2023 and participated in several subsequent funding rounds. [...] Sound Ventures’ stake is likely now worth billions of dollars after the company’s lofty IPO.

The seven co-founders:

> Each co-founder sold 3.38 million shares in the IPO, according to the prospectus, for total proceeds of $1.96 billion.

The institutional investors:

> Anthropic prioritized allocating shares to institutional investors it had relationships with, according to people familiar with the details, who asked not to be named because they were not authorized to speak publicly.

The employees:

> The average Anthropic employee will be worth over $40 million after today’s IPO, marking one of the most rapid wealth creation events in history. [...] Earlier hires stand to benefit even further, with over 400 employees expected to be worth $100 million or more at the current share price.

Even the retail investors:

> Retail allocation came in at around 15%, the people said.

But there’s a slight catch for retail investors lucky enough to get in at the IPO price who want to cash out:

> Retail investors with access to the Anthropic IPO who try to sell the stock for a quick profit may face a penalty from their brokerage.
>
> Anthropic made shares available to retail investors through brokerages including Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi, E-Trade and some of their respective affiliates, the AI startup said in an IPO prospectus.
>
> Many of them have “anti-flipping” policies meant to discourage investors who purchase IPO shares from dumping them fast.
>
> These policies apply only to retail investors who place an order with their brokerage firm to buy the Anthropic IPO at its offering price — something many investors don’t have access to.
>
> For example, Fidelity clients who sell IPO shares within the first 15 calendar days from the start of trading in the secondary market would be barred from participating in future stock IPOs through the brokerage.

At today's closing price, retail investors who received shares at the IPO price would collectively be sitting on a paper gain of around US$2.08 billion, assuming they still held those shares at the close. 

To put that into perspective, US$2.08 billion amounts to around 25 US cents per person if it were spread evenly across the global population, which it is not.
